2026 Senate and governor candidates

Sourced explainer · Checked October 22, 2026 · Election day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026

This page lists every candidate on the ballot for US Senate and governor this year, plus the recent events that changed these races. It does not endorse anyone. States are in alphabetical order, and so are the two major parties. Other candidates are sorted by last name.

How this was checked: Every Democratic and Republican nominee was found in three independent sources: the race’s Wikipedia article, 270toWin’s candidate data, and Ballotpedia’s general election listing. The “Also on the ballot” column comes from Ballotpedia, which tracks who qualified for the ballot. Write-in candidates and running mates are not listed. Where sources disagree, the notes say so. Candidates can withdraw late, so confirm with your sample ballot before you vote.

Recent political updates

Events in 2026 that changed who is on the ballot, newest first.

  1. October 18 Massachusetts. Shawn Oliver, the Republican candidate for lieutenant governor and Mike Minogue’s running mate, suspended his campaign after a crash and arrest. UPI
  2. September 9 Rhode Island. Governor Dan McKee lost the Democratic primary to Helena Foulkes. He is the first elected governor to lose renomination since 2014.
  3. August 25 South Carolina. Appointed Senator Darline Graham won the Republican special runoff over Representative Ralph Norman.
  4. August 25 Oklahoma. N’Kiyla Jasmine Thomas won the Democratic Senate runoff.
  5. August 18 Florida. Angie Nixon beat Alex Vindman for the Democratic Senate nomination in an upset.
  6. August 11 Wisconsin. David Crowley won the Democratic primary for governor over Francesca Hong.
  7. August 4 South Dakota. Democratic Senate nominee Julian Beaudion withdrew, leaving independent Brian Bengs as Senator Mike Rounds’s main opponent. Ballotpedia News
  8. August 4 Michigan. Abdul El-Sayed won the Democratic Senate primary.
  9. July 28 Idaho. Democratic Senate nominee David Roth withdrew after Democratic leaders backed independent Todd Achilles.
  10. July 25 Maine. A Democratic convention chose Troy Jackson as the Senate nominee.
  11. July 11 South Carolina. Senator Lindsey Graham died, weeks after winning the Republican primary.
  12. July 10 Maine. Democratic Senate nominee Graham Platner withdrew after sexual assault accusations and other controversies.
  13. July Nebraska. Democratic Senate nominee Cindy Burbank withdrew to support independent Dan Osborn.
  14. June 27 Louisiana. Julia Letlow won the Republican Senate runoff. Senator Bill Cassidy had finished third in the May 16 primary.
  15. May 26 Texas. Ken Paxton beat Senator John Cornyn in the Republican runoff.
  16. March Oklahoma. Senator Markwayne Mullin resigned to become secretary of homeland security. Alan Armstrong was appointed to the seat.
  17. March Montana. Senator Steve Daines withdrew minutes before the filing deadline and endorsed Kurt Alme.
  18. January Minnesota. Governor Tim Walz ended his bid for a third term. Amy Klobuchar later won the Democratic nomination.

House and local races

All 435 House seats and thousands of state and local offices are also on the ballot. These free, nonpartisan tools show the exact candidates on your own ballot:

  • VOTE411, from the League of Women Voters. Enter your address for a personal voter guide with candidates' own answers.
  • Ballotpedia's sample ballot lookup. It covers federal, state, and many local races.
  • vote.gov, the official US government site, for registration and your state's election office.
  • Your county or state election office, which publishes the official sample ballot.

What you can do with this

Look up your own ballot, then read each candidate's own website and a news source you trust. If something on this page is wrong or out of date, report it with a link to an official source.
