# Writing guide

This guide is for writing posts for blog.hoto.lol (Planet Hoto) and Hotolosophy. It is not part of the site. Files at the repo root are not published by GitHub Pages, which serves only `docs/`.

## Before you publish

Ask: does this sound like me, help someone understand something real, and protect people's dignity?

## The filter for every post

1. **Why do I care about this?** Name the value or lived experience behind it.
2. **What kind of post is it?** Label it as personal reflection, opinion, sourced explainer, or guest perspective.
3. **What is the real issue?** Critique a policy, claim, behavior, or system, not a whole identity group.
4. **What supports the point?** Link the source, say what is uncertain, and include the strongest relevant counterpoint. Do not present weak evidence as equal to strong evidence.
5. **What can readers do with it?** Offer a useful next step, or invite thoughtful disagreement.

## Where posts go

- **blog.hoto.lol (Planet Hoto)** can hold personal arguments and a faith lens.
- **Hotolosophy** stays focused on free, practical education and resources.

## Three shelves

Every post sits on exactly one shelf, and its label on the page says which.

- **What I believe**: opinion. Say plainly what I think, label it as my view, and give the values behind it.
- **What I'm learning**: sourced explainer. Link sources, mark what is uncertain, and include the strongest counterpoint.
- **Ideas for change**: proposal. Name one specific change, who it could help, who might bear a cost, and what result would make me rethink it.

## Post frame

Use this when it fits. Skip lines that don't.

1. My point is…
2. Why this matters to me…
3. Here's what the evidence supports…
4. Who could be affected, and what might I be missing?
5. Here's one step I want to take.

Strong conviction is fine. Don't invent a "both sides" balance where the evidence isn't balanced.

## Before publishing

- [ ] Facts are sourced, and the post says what is uncertain.
- [ ] Opinion is labeled as opinion, and fact is labeled as fact.
- [ ] I'm criticizing a policy, claim, or action, not a person's identity.
- [ ] I'm not treating one person as if they represent a whole group.
- [ ] I have permission to share any story that could identify someone.
- [ ] I can correct this visibly if I get something wrong, and I know where the correction will go.
